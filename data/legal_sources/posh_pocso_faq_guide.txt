POSH & POCSO: Frequently Asked Queries and Section-Referenced Answers
Compiled strictly from the text of the POCSO Act, 2012, the POSH Act, 2013, and their official complaint formats

Part A — POCSO Act, 2012

A.1 General & Offence Categories
Query: What is the POCSO Act and what does it cover?
Answer: The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 is an Act to protect children from sexual assault, sexual harassment and pornography, and to provide for Special Courts to try such offences.
Section Reference: Preamble

Query: Who is considered a "child" under the Act?
Answer: Any person below the age of eighteen years.
Section Reference: Sec. 2(1)(d)

Query: What are the categories of sexual offences against children recognised by the Act?
Answer: (A) Penetrative sexual assault, (B) aggravated penetrative sexual assault, (C) sexual assault, (D) aggravated sexual assault, and (E) sexual harassment — plus using a child for pornographic purposes as a separate chapter.
Section Reference: Ch. II & III

Query: What is "penetrative sexual assault"?
Answer: It includes penile, object, or body-part penetration of the child's vagina, mouth, urethra or anus (or the child being made to do so with another person), manipulation causing such penetration, or oral contact with the child's sexual organs.
Section Reference: Sec. 3

Query: What is the punishment for penetrative sexual assault?
Answer: Imprisonment of either description for not less than ten years, which may extend to imprisonment for life, plus fine. If the child is below sixteen years, the minimum is twenty years, extendable to imprisonment for life (meaning the remainder of natural life), plus fine. The fine must be just and reasonable and paid to the victim for medical expenses and rehabilitation.
Section Reference: Sec. 4

Query: What makes penetrative sexual assault "aggravated"?
Answer: Circumstances such as the offender being a police officer, member of armed/security forces, public servant, staff of a jail/hospital/educational or religious institution; gang assault; use of deadly weapons; causing grievous hurt, pregnancy, HIV infection, mental illness or death; assault on a child below twelve years; assault by a relative or person in a position of trust/authority; repeated assault; assault during communal/sectarian violence or a natural calamity; prior conviction for a similar offence; or making the child strip/parade naked in public.
Section Reference: Sec. 5

Query: What is the punishment for aggravated penetrative sexual assault?
Answer: Rigorous imprisonment for not less than twenty years, extendable to imprisonment for life (remainder of natural life), plus fine, or death. The fine must be just and reasonable and paid to the victim for medical expenses and rehabilitation.
Section Reference: Sec. 6

Query: What is "sexual assault" (as distinct from penetrative sexual assault)?
Answer: Touching the vagina, penis, anus or breast of the child with sexual intent (or making the child touch such parts of the accused or another person), or any other act with sexual intent involving physical contact without penetration.
Section Reference: Sec. 7

Query: What is the punishment for sexual assault?
Answer: Imprisonment of either description for not less than three years, which may extend to five years, plus fine.
Section Reference: Sec. 8

Query: What is the punishment for aggravated sexual assault?
Answer: Imprisonment of either description for not less than five years, which may extend to seven years, plus fine. Aggravating circumstances mirror those listed for aggravated penetrative sexual assault, with an additional circumstance covering administration of drugs/hormones/chemical substances to induce early sexual maturity.
Section Reference: Sec. 9 & 10

Query: What amounts to "sexual harassment" of a child under POCSO?
Answer: With sexual intent: uttering words/sounds or making gestures/exhibiting body parts meant to be heard or seen by the child; making a child exhibit their body; showing pornographic material to a child; repeatedly following, watching or contacting a child; threatening to use a real or fabricated depiction of the child in media; or enticing a child for pornographic purposes or gratification.
Section Reference: Sec. 11

Query: What is the punishment for sexual harassment of a child?
Answer: Imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, plus fine.
Section Reference: Sec. 12

Query: What does the Act say about using a child for pornographic purposes?
Answer: Using a child in any media/print/electronic form for sexual gratification (including representation of sexual organs, real or simulated sexual acts, or indecent/obscene representation) is an offence. Punishment is imprisonment not less than five years plus fine, rising to not less than seven years on a second or subsequent conviction. If the accused also directly participates in the underlying sexual offence, the punishments under the relevant assault sections apply in addition.
Section Reference: Sec. 13 & 14

Query: Is mere storage/possession of child pornographic material an offence?
Answer: Yes. Failing to delete/destroy/report such material with intent to share attracts a fine (₹5,000 minimum, ₹10,000 for repeat offence). Storing/possessing for transmission/distribution attracts up to three years' imprisonment or fine or both. Storing/possessing for commercial purposes attracts three to five years' imprisonment (first conviction) and five to seven years (subsequent conviction), plus fine.
Section Reference: Sec. 15


A.2 Reporting Obligations & False Complaints
Query: Who must report a POCSO offence, and to whom?
Answer: Any person (including the child) who apprehends that an offence is likely to be committed, or has knowledge that one has been committed, must give the information to the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) or the local police — not to an internal workplace or institutional committee.
Section Reference: Sec. 19(1)

Query: What happens after a report is filed with the police/SJPU?
Answer: The report is given an entry number, recorded in writing, read over to the informant, and entered in a register. If the child needs care and protection, the police must, after recording reasons in writing, arrange care (including shelter home or hospital admission) within 24 hours, and must report the matter to the Child Welfare Committee and the Special Court (or Court of Session) within 24 hours.
Section Reference: Sec. 19(2)–(6)

Query: Do media, hotels, hospitals, clubs, studios or photo facilities have a separate reporting duty?
Answer: Yes. On coming across any material or object that is sexually exploitative of a child, such personnel must inform the SJPU or local police.
Section Reference: Sec. 20

Query: What is the penalty for failing to report or record a POCSO offence?
Answer: Imprisonment up to six months, or fine, or both. A person in charge of a company/institution who fails to report an offence concerning a subordinate under their control faces imprisonment up to one year and fine. This liability does not apply to a child.
Section Reference: Sec. 21

Query: What if a POCSO complaint turns out to be false?
Answer: A person who makes a false complaint or gives false information solely to humiliate, extort, threaten or defame someone faces imprisonment up to six months, or fine, or both. No punishment applies if the false complaint/information was made by a child. A person who knowingly makes a false complaint against a child, victimising the child, faces imprisonment up to one year, or fine, or both.
Section Reference: Sec. 22

Query: Can the media report on a child's identity in a POCSO case?
Answer: No. No report may disclose the child's identity (name, address, photograph, family details, school, neighbourhood, or any particular that could reveal identity), and no comments may be made without complete, authentic information. The Special Court may permit disclosure only for recorded reasons, if in the child's interest.
Section Reference: Sec. 23

Query: Should a POCSO matter be routed through an organisation's internal committee?
Answer: No. Per the mandatory reporting notice on the POCSO complaint format, a POCSO report must be filed with the police or SJPU, not an internal workplace committee.
Section Reference: POCSO Complaint Format Mandatory Reporting Notice


A.3 Procedure, Special Courts & Trial Safeguards
Query: How is a child's statement recorded by police?
Answer: As far as practicable, at the child's residence or place of choice, by a woman police officer not below sub-inspector rank, who must not be in uniform. The child must not come into contact with the accused during the process, and no child may be detained at a police station overnight. The child's identity must be protected from the media unless the Special Court directs otherwise in the child's interest.
Section Reference: Sec. 24

Query: How is a child's statement recorded by a Magistrate?
Answer: The Magistrate records the statement as spoken by the child under Section 164 CrPC, but the proviso allowing the accused's advocate to be present does not apply. A copy of the Section 207 CrPC document must be given to the child/parents once the police file the final report.
Section Reference: Sec. 25

Query: Who may be present, and what assistance is available, while a child's statement is recorded?
Answer: The statement is recorded in the presence of the child's parents or another trusted person. A translator/interpreter or, for a child with a disability, a special educator/expert may assist. Wherever possible, the statement is also recorded by audio-video means.
Section Reference: Sec. 26

Query: How is a child medically examined under POCSO?
Answer: In accordance with Section 164A CrPC, even without a registered FIR/complaint. A girl child must be examined by a woman doctor, in the presence of a parent or trusted person (or, if unavailable, a woman nominated by the head of the medical institution).
Section Reference: Sec. 27

Query: What is a Special Court and what protections does it provide the child during trial?
Answer: A Court of Session designated by the State Government to try POCSO offences. It must allow a trusted family member/guardian/friend to be present, avoid repeated appearances by the child, disallow aggressive questioning or character assassination, protect the child's dignity, ensure the child does not see the accused while testifying (e.g., via video-conferencing, screens or curtains), and try the case in camera. It may also direct compensation for the child.
Section Reference: Sec. 28, 33, 35

Query: Is there a presumption in favour of the prosecution in POCSO trials?
Answer: Yes. Where a person is prosecuted for committing, abetting, or attempting an offence under Sections 3, 5, 7 or 9, the Special Court presumes the offence was committed unless the contrary is proved by the accused. A culpable mental state is also presumed, subject to the accused proving otherwise beyond reasonable doubt.
Section Reference: Sec. 29 & 30

Query: What are the timelines for evidence and trial in a POCSO case?
Answer: The child's evidence should be recorded within thirty days of the Special Court taking cognizance (reasons for delay to be recorded), and the trial should be completed, as far as possible, within one year from the date of cognizance.
Section Reference: Sec. 35

Query: Can a child get free legal assistance in a POCSO case?
Answer: The child's family/guardian is entitled to a legal counsel of their choice; if they cannot afford one, the Legal Services Authority must provide a lawyer.
Section Reference: Sec. 40

Query: What happens if the same act is punishable both under POCSO and under the IPC/IT Act?
Answer: The offender is liable to be punished only under whichever law (POCSO or the specified IPC/IT Act provisions) provides the greater punishment — not under both.
Section Reference: Sec. 42


Part B — POSH Act, 2013

B.1 General & Scope
Query: What is the POSH Act and what does it cover?
Answer: The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 provides protection against sexual harassment of women at the workplace and lays down a mechanism for prevention and redressal of complaints.
Section Reference: Preamble

Query: Who is an "aggrieved woman" under the Act?
Answer: In relation to a workplace, a woman of any age, whether employed or not, who alleges she has been subjected to an act of sexual harassment by the respondent. In relation to a dwelling place/house, a woman of any age employed there.
Section Reference: Sec. 2(a)

Query: What counts as a "workplace"?
Answer: It is defined broadly to include government and private establishments of any kind carrying on commercial, professional, vocational, educational, entertainment, industrial, health-service or financial activity; hospitals/nursing homes; sports institutes/stadiums/complexes/venues; any place visited by the employee arising out of or during employment (including employer-provided transport); and a dwelling place or house (for domestic workers).
Section Reference: Sec. 2(o)

Query: What conduct amounts to "sexual harassment" under POSH?
Answer: Unwelcome acts or behaviour (directly or by implication) such as physical contact and advances; a demand or request for sexual favours; sexually coloured remarks; showing pornography; or any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.
Section Reference: Sec. 2(n)

Query: What circumstances can turn such conduct into actionable sexual harassment?
Answer: An implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment in employment; an implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment; a threat about present or future employment status; interference with work or creation of an intimidating, offensive or hostile work environment; or humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.
Section Reference: Sec. 3(2)


B.2 Internal Committee & Local Committee
Query: Is every employer required to set up a committee?
Answer: Yes. Every employer must, by a written order, constitute an Internal Committee (IC). Where offices/units are located at different places or at divisional/sub-divisional level, an IC must be constituted at each such unit.
Section Reference: Sec. 4(1)

Query: Who sits on the Internal Committee?
Answer: A Presiding Officer (a woman employed at a senior level, or nominated from another unit/workplace of the same employer if no senior woman is available); at least two members from among employees, preferably committed to women's causes or with social-work/legal experience; and one member from an NGO/association committed to women's causes or familiar with sexual harassment issues. At least half the nominated members must be women.
Section Reference: Sec. 4(2)

Query: How long does an IC member serve?
Answer: For a period not exceeding three years from the date of nomination, as specified by the employer.
Section Reference: Sec. 4(3)

Query: When is a Local Committee (LC) involved instead of the IC?
Answer: Where the workplace has fewer than ten workers (so no IC is required) or where the complaint is against the employer himself, the complaint goes to the Local Committee constituted by the District Officer for that district.
Section Reference: Sec. 6(1)

Query: Who sits on the Local Committee?
Answer: A Chairperson (an eminent woman in social work committed to women's causes); one member from women working in the local block/taluka/tehsil/ward/municipality; two members (at least one a woman) from NGOs/associations committed to women's causes, with a preference for legal background and a requirement that at least one nominee be a woman from SC/ST/OBC or a notified minority community; and the local social welfare/women & child development officer as an ex-officio member.
Section Reference: Sec. 7(1)


B.3 Complaint, Conciliation & Inquiry Process
Query: How and when must a POSH complaint be filed?
Answer: The aggrieved woman must make a written complaint to the IC (or LC, if no IC exists) within three months of the incident, or within three months of the last incident in a series. The Committee may extend this by a further period not exceeding three months, for recorded reasons, if satisfied the circumstances prevented timely filing.
Section Reference: Sec. 9(1)

Query: What if the woman cannot write the complaint herself?
Answer: The Presiding Officer/Chairperson or a Committee member must render reasonable assistance to help her put the complaint in writing.
Section Reference: Sec. 9(1), proviso

Query: Can someone else file the complaint on her behalf?
Answer: Yes, if she is unable to complain due to physical or mental incapacity, death, or otherwise, her legal heir or another prescribed person may file the complaint.
Section Reference: Sec. 9(2)

Query: Is conciliation available before a formal inquiry?
Answer: Yes, at the aggrieved woman's request, before an inquiry is initiated — but no monetary settlement may form the basis of conciliation. If a settlement is reached, no further inquiry is conducted, and the recorded settlement is forwarded to the employer/District Officer and copies given to both parties.
Section Reference: Sec. 10

Query: What is the timeline for the inquiry itself?
Answer: The inquiry must be completed within ninety days.
Section Reference: Sec. 11(4)

Query: What if the respondent is a domestic worker's employer or the matter involves a domestic worker complaint?
Answer: Where the respondent is a domestic worker, the Local Committee, if a prima facie case exists, must forward the complaint to the police within seven days for registration under Section 509 IPC and other applicable provisions.
Section Reference: Sec. 11(1)

Query: What powers does the Committee have during an inquiry?
Answer: The same powers as a civil court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, for summoning and enforcing attendance and examining a person on oath, and requiring discovery/production of documents.
Section Reference: Sec. 11(3)

Query: Can interim relief be granted while the inquiry is pending?
Answer: Yes, on the aggrieved woman's written request, the Committee may recommend that the employer transfer the aggrieved woman or the respondent, grant the aggrieved woman leave of up to three months (in addition to her other leave entitlement), or grant other prescribed relief.
Section Reference: Sec. 12

Query: What happens once the inquiry is complete?
Answer: The Committee submits its findings to the employer/District Officer within ten days of completing the inquiry, and the report is made available to the parties. If the allegation is not proved, the Committee recommends no action. If proved, it recommends action for misconduct under applicable service rules and/or a deduction from the respondent's salary/wages payable to the aggrieved woman, determined per Section 15 factors. The employer/District Officer must act on the recommendation within sixty days.
Section Reference: Sec. 13

Query: What factors determine the compensation amount?
Answer: Mental trauma, pain, suffering and emotional distress caused; loss of career opportunity; medical expenses for physical or psychiatric treatment; the respondent's income and financial status; and the feasibility of lump-sum or instalment payment.
Section Reference: Sec. 15

Query: What if the complaint is found to be false or malicious?
Answer: The Committee may recommend action against the woman/complainant under applicable service rules — but a mere inability to substantiate the complaint or provide adequate proof is not, by itself, grounds for action; malicious intent must be established through an inquiry before any action is recommended. False evidence or forged documents by a witness can similarly attract recommended action against that witness.
Section Reference: Sec. 14

Query: Can proceedings/complaint contents be published or disclosed?
Answer: No. The contents of the complaint, the identity/addresses of the aggrieved woman, respondent and witnesses, conciliation/inquiry information, Committee recommendations, and action taken must not be published or made known to the public, press or media — this overrides the Right to Information Act, 2005. Information about justice secured may be disseminated without revealing identifying particulars.
Section Reference: Sec. 16

Query: What is the penalty for breaching this confidentiality requirement?
Answer: A person entrusted with handling the complaint/inquiry/recommendation who contravenes Section 16 is liable to a penalty under applicable service rules (or as prescribed, where no service rules exist).
Section Reference: Sec. 17

Query: Can the Committee's recommendation be appealed?
Answer: Yes, within ninety days of the recommendation, to the court or tribunal in accordance with applicable service rules, or otherwise as prescribed.
Section Reference: Sec. 18


B.4 Employer Duties & Penalties
Query: What are an employer's core duties under POSH?
Answer: Provide a safe working environment; display the IC's constitution order and penal consequences of sexual harassment at a conspicuous place; organise awareness workshops and IC-member orientation; provide facilities/assistance for the inquiry (including securing attendance of respondent/witnesses and furnishing required information); assist the woman in filing a complaint under the IPC or other law if she wishes; initiate action under the IPC/other law against the perpetrator; treat sexual harassment as misconduct under service rules; and monitor timely submission of IC reports.
Section Reference: Sec. 19

Query: What must the employer include in its annual report?
Answer: The number of cases filed, if any, and their disposal under the Act — or, where no annual report is otherwise required, intimation of such numbers to the District Officer.
Section Reference: Sec. 22

Query: What is the penalty if an employer fails to comply with the Act?
Answer: A fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees for failing to constitute an IC, failing to act under Sections 13/14/22, or contravening/attempting to contravene/abetting contravention of other provisions. A repeat conviction attracts up to twice the earlier punishment (subject to the maximum prescribed) and may lead to cancellation, withdrawal or non-renewal of the licence/registration needed to carry on the business.
Section Reference: Sec. 26

Query: Which courts can take cognizance of a POSH offence, and how?
Answer: Only on a complaint made by the aggrieved woman or a person authorised by the IC/LC in this behalf; no court inferior to a Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the first class may try such an offence; and every offence under the Act is non-cognizable.
Section Reference: Sec. 27


Part C — Cross-Cutting Queries (POSH & POCSO Together)
Query: If a complaint involves a minor (a person under 18) at a workplace, does it go to the Internal Committee or the police?
Answer: POCSO's mandatory reporting requirement directs any person with knowledge of an offence against a child to report to the police/SJPU, not to an internal committee; the POCSO complaint format's mandatory reporting notice states this explicitly. POSH separately defines "aggrieved woman" as a woman of any age, so a workplace complaint by/about a minor woman could also engage POSH mechanisms. Neither Act, as provided, states a single rule resolving which process takes precedence in every situation — organisations should treat this as a matter for legal guidance rather than assumption.
Section Reference: POCSO Sec. 19, POCSO Complaint Format Notice, POSH Sec. 2(a)

Query: Are POSH and POCSO proceedings confidential in similar ways?
Answer: Both Acts protect identity: POSH bars publication of the complaint, identities and proceedings (Section 16); POCSO bars media disclosure of the child's identity (Section 23) and requires the Special Court to protect the child's identity during investigation/trial (Section 33(7)).
Section Reference: POSH Sec. 16, POCSO Sec. 23 & 33(7)

Query: Do both Acts penalise false complaints, and in the same way?
Answer: Both address false complaints, but differently. Under POSH, the Committee can recommend service-rule action against a complainant found to have knowingly made a false or malicious complaint (Section 14), subject to safeguards. Under POCSO, false complaints/information given solely to humiliate, extort, threaten or defame attract imprisonment up to six months or fine or both (Section 22(1)); no punishment applies where a child made the false complaint (Section 22(2)); and a person who knowingly makes a false complaint against a child faces imprisonment up to one year or fine or both (Section 22(3)).
Section Reference: POSH Sec. 14, POCSO Sec. 22
